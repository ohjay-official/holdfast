The problem

In Lagos, robbers who take a phone often make the owner unlock it and transfer their balance on the spot. In September 2025 one victim lost ₦2.6 million from his bank app after a robbery at gunpoint, and his post asking for help was viewed millions of times (WeeTracker). Lagos commuters also face "one chance" gangs, who rob passengers in vehicles that pose as public transport (The African Mirror).

Crypto holders face the same thing worldwide. The security firm CertiK counted 72 verified "wrench attacks", where people are forced to hand over crypto, in 2025. That was up 75% on 2024, and many more go unreported (Decrypt).

A crypto payment can't be reversed, and with self-custody there's no bank to call afterwards.

Who it's for first

People who keep savings in Bitcoin Cash on their phone and live or travel where street robbery is common. Paytaca, the wallet Holdfast works with, is built for everyday payments, so its users carry their wallet everywhere.

The same need applies to anyone with savings in a phone app, crypto or not. Bitcoin Cash makes it possible without a bank: the contract enforces the rules, and fees are fractions of a cent.

How it pays for itself

A small one-time fee when you create a vault on mainnet, paid in BCH in the same transaction. No subscription, and Holdfast never holds your money.

Later: alerts on Telegram when a withdrawal starts, and a guardian service for people who don't have someone to ask.
